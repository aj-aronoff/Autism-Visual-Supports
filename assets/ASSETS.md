# Autism Visual Supports — asset notes

Every image here is original vector art, generated by `build_art.py` and rasterized
with headless Chrome. Nothing is stock, so there is no attribution or licence
obligation — which matters, because the hackathon rules require the project to be
open-source.

Edit `build_art.py`, then re-render:

```bash
python3 build_art.py
CHROME="/mnt/c/Program Files/Google/Chrome/Application/chrome.exe"
B='C:\users\ajaro\claude\resume\hackathon-future\assets'
"$CHROME" --headless --disable-gpu --no-sandbox --hide-scrollbars \
  --window-size=1600,900 --screenshot="$B\cover-v1-storybook.png" "$B\cover-v1-storybook.svg"
```

Add `--default-background-color=00000000` for the transparent badges.

### Do not use

- Bright red `#FF0000` — alarming in child-facing contexts
- Neon or fluorescent anything — sensory overload risk
- Pure black `#000000` — harsh on white; use Deep Slate
- Sunflower or HDS Yellow **as a text colour** — fill only, always

## The one rule that is not negotiable

**The same child appears in the FIRST card and the THEN card.** Always. A visual
support works by being predictable, and a child who does not recognise themselves
across the two cards has been handed two unrelated pictures instead of one sequence.

`child_brushing()`, `child_swinging()` and `child_reading()` therefore share the same
defaults — same skin, same hair, same shirt, same trousers. Change one and change all
three. `child_swinging(ponytail=True)` exists for when a *second*, different child is
genuinely wanted; it is off by default and should stay off inside any one board.

## The figure-to-card ratio

A card's picture must hold the same share of the card at every size, or the child
shrinks to a speck as soon as the board moves onto a tablet. The full-size board is
the reference:

| | figure / card height | figure / card width |
|---|---|---|
| Full board (`card()`, 317×368 card) | **0.766** | 0.889 |
| Tablet screen, before the fix | 0.707 | 0.713 |
| Tablet screen, after | **0.767** | 0.844 |

Every pad inside `board_screen()` is therefore a fraction of the card — header band,
label size, picture inset, label lift — never a pixel count. Fixed padding was the
whole bug: numbers tuned for a 368px card swamped a 104px one.

**Thin strokes do not survive the same trip.** The swing's ropes are 5 units in a
200-unit figure; at a card scale of 0.4 that renders under 2px and the swing simply
disappears. `child_swinging(lw=...)` thickens the ropes and seat, and `board_screen()`
sets `lw = clamp(0.8 / scale, 1.0, 2.5)` so they stay visible at any size without
looking clumsy on the full board.

## Cautions, learned the hard way

From comparing Symbol Library v3.1 against v3.2 in the Calm Skies work, plus two
mistakes made and fixed while generating this set.

1. **Keep the circle badge.** In v3.2 the Airport and Plane marks lost their badges
   and became bare glyphs floating on white. The badge is the system; without it the
   icon is just a picture.
2. **A transparent or white PNG disappears.** `book.png` in the DivergentTravel icon
   set is pure white — invisible on any light background. v3.2's Rehearsal icon
   rendered completely blank for the same reason. Always check an icon against the
   surface it will actually sit on.
3. **Do not hand-roll figures into an icon-font set.** v3.2 swapped clean Font
   Awesome glyphs for custom SVG stick figures, and the two visual languages fought
   each other. Either commit to the icon font or commit to illustration — not both
   in one row.
4. **Green on blue fails contrast.** v3.2 put HDS green figures on Calm Sky Blue
   badges. Pair a saturated figure with a light badge, or a white figure with a
   saturated one.
5. **Draw order decides what is visible.** The first pass of `child_brushing()` put
   the raised arm and toothbrush *before* the head, so both rendered behind it and
   the child appeared to be doing nothing. The brush is the entire point of the card.
6. **Check text width at the rendered size.** The first covers ran the title off the
   right edge. Render and look; do not assume it fits.
7. **Type over artwork needs a cleared lane.** The first tree cover ran its subtitle
   straight through the digital cloud. Move the art, not the words.
8. **A held object will eat the body holding it.** The first tablet covers put the
   slab so high and so large that the child became a floating head with two detached
   hands. Hold the device low, keep it smaller than instinct says, and leave the chest
   and both arms visible.
9. **Text inside artwork has to be measured, not guessed.** "Brush teeth" overflowed
   its card once the board was shrunk onto a tablet screen. `board_screen()` now sizes
   each label to its card width instead of scaling it with the card height.
10. **A limb the same colour as the torso is not a limb.** The tablet child's arms
    melted into his shirt until the sleeves took a second tone (`CLEAR_SKY` against
    `SKY`) and bowed outward far enough to show beside the chest before meeting the
    hands. Draw the sleeves *before* the torso so they tuck under the shoulder line.
12. **A banner upload gets cropped more than once.** Taikai shows the 1360x255 cover
    full width on the project page but centre-crops the *same file* to about 490x255
    on the projects-list card, and drops the round avatar over roughly x 461-573,
    y 200-255 of that crop. The first banner put the title at x=300 and the child at
    x=980, so the card showed nothing but sliced text. Treat **x 450-910 as the safe
    zone**: the one image that has to survive alone goes dead centre, text and cloud
    go in the wings. Verified clean for crop widths 380-700.

11. **Keep one copy of the generator.** A stray `cp` overwrote the newer `build_art.py`
    with an older one and silently dropped a whole block of work. `assets/build_art.py`
    is the only source of truth; copy *from* it, never *to* it.

## Icon source, if you add icons

The DivergentTravel set is Font Awesome 6 Free (Solid), 256px, white glyph on a
coloured circle badge. Font Awesome Free is **CC BY 4.0**, which requires attribution
— put `Icons: Font Awesome (CC BY 4.0)` in the site footer if you use them.

## Free stock, if original art ever will not do

Unsplash · Pexels · Pixabay · Burst by Shopify · RawPixel · Canva for Nonprofits
(free Canva Pro for registered nonprofits — worth asking the partner org about).

Prefer original vector anyway: stock photographs of children carry model-release and
licensing questions that an open-source project should not inherit, and the ASD
design rules want clutter-free flat art rather than photographs.
